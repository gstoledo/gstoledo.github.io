<strong>Abstract:</strong> In the aftermath of conﬂict, how should history be communicated to promote long-run peace? We develop a dynamic model in which rival groups repeatedly engage
in conﬂict games with unknown, evolving costs revealed only through conﬂict. The
optimal communication policy balances the likelihood of immediate peace against its
persistence. More informative communication sustains longer periods of peace, whereas
less informative communication increases the chance of immediate peace but shortens
its duration. The optimal policy depends on the persistence of the environment, with
less informative communication preferred in more unstable settings. Our framework
provides insights into post-conﬂict communication, with implications for transitional
justice and peace agreements.
